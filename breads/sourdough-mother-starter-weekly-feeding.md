# Sourdough Starter - Weekly Maintenance and Baking

**Category:** Breads / Techniques  
**Status:** Established maintenance method; 🧪 Testing 50/50 einkorn feeding flour  
**Temperature:** 78°F (countertop fermentation appliance)  
**Maintenance frequency:** Once a week when not baking

## Start Here: Choose Your Path

| Your goal | What you do | Where it ends |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Keep the starter alive (no baking)** | Feed the **mother starter** at 1:2:2 | Put it back in the refrigerator for about a week |
| **B. Bake bread** | Follow your **bread recipe** for starter or levain preparation | Bake as directed; keep some mother starter for next time |

**Mother starter** = the starter you keep and maintain from week to week.  
**Levain** = a separate, freshly fed portion of starter used when a bread recipe calls for one.  
**Golden rule:** Never put all of your mother starter into bread dough. Keep some for future feedings.

## Path A - Weekly Maintenance (Not Baking)

Follow this path if you simply want to keep your starter healthy until another day.

### Ingredients - 100 g total

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Existing mother starter | 20 g |
| Bread flour | 20 g |
| Jovial whole wheat einkorn flour | 20 g |
| Water | 40 g |
| **Total** | **100 g** |

**Feeding ratio: 1:2:2** means **1 part existing starter : 2 parts total flour : 2 parts water**. The 50/50 flour blend is an experiment, not a requirement. You may instead use 40 g of your usual feeding flour.

### Steps

1. Remove the mother starter from the refrigerator.
2. Keep **20 g** in a clean jar. Set aside or discard the remainder.
3. Stir in **40 g water**, then **20 g bread flour** and **20 g whole wheat einkorn**. Mix until no dry flour remains.
4. Mark the starting level on the jar. Cover appropriately without sealing it airtight.
5. Place in the countertop fermentation appliance at **78°F**.
6. **Begin checking after 3 hours.** Expect to assess it around **3-4 hours**, but use visible activity, not just the clock. Look for bubbles and a substantial rise, with a domed or just-leveling surface.
7. Refrigerate while active, preferably near peak and before major collapse.
8. Leave refrigerated until next week's feeding, or until you need starter for baking.

**Done. You do not need to make bread or build a levain.**

## Path B - Baking Bread

When you plan to bake, **follow the instructions in your chosen bread recipe**. The recipe will tell you how much starter or levain to prepare, how to feed it, when it is ready, and how to make the bread.

**Important:** Keep some of your mother starter for future use. Do not add the entire jar to your dough. If your reserved mother starter needs feeding, follow **Path A** before returning it to the refrigerator.

**You do not need to use Path A's 3-4-hour maintenance window for baking.** The bread recipe determines the timing.

## If You Forget the Starter in the Appliance

If it rises and then collapses, or becomes watery, it is often simply overripe. If it smells normally sour or yeasty and shows no contamination, you can usually feed it again; an occasional collapse does not mean the starter is dead. For a routine weekly feed, refrigeration after a modest overshoot is generally fine. If it is very runny and exhausted, refresh it before relying on it to raise bread.

**Discard the entire starter if it develops fuzzy mold or pink/orange discoloration.**

## Storage and Troubleshooting

- **Refrigerated:** Maintain approximately weekly according to your usual routine.
- **At 78°F:** Check the 50/50 einkorn blend at 3 hours; it may behave differently from all-bread-flour feedings.
- **Starter looks bubbly but does not double:** Einkorn may retain gas differently. Judge activity from multiple signs rather than height alone.
- **Ready to bake but starter seems weak:** Give the mother starter a fresh feeding and wait until it is active before building a levain.
- **Unsure which path to use:** If you are **not baking**, choose **Path A**. If you **are baking**, choose **Path B**.
