# Traeger Flatrock 28-Inch Griddle: Cleaning & Maintenance

**Category:** Techniques  
**Equipment:** Traeger Flatrock 28-inch griddle  
**Purpose:** Cleaning, seasoning, and preventing residue transfer to food

## Recommended Tools

- Smooth-edged metal griddle scraper
- Clean cotton bar towels (and heat-resistant gloves or tongs)
- Water in a squeeze bottle
- Grapeseed oil
- Paper towels for applying oil, if preferred

## Cleaning After Every Use

1. While the griddle is still warm, scrape food scraps and loose residue toward the grease trap.
2. Apply a **small amount of water** to stubborn spots to loosen cooked-on residue. Watch for steam and splatter.
3. Scrape again, then carefully wipe with a clean cotton towel held with tongs or other heat-safe protection.
4. Repeat with small amounts of water until visible residue is removed.
5. Allow the surface to dry. Apply a **very thin coat of grapeseed oil**, wiping away any pools.
6. Let the griddle cool before covering. Empty and clean the grease trap once safe to handle.

**Safety:** Do not pour large amounts of cold water onto a very hot griddle. Follow Traeger's care instructions.

## Stubborn Grease and Carbonized Residue

1. Warm the griddle enough to soften residue, then reduce or turn off heat as needed for safe cleaning.
2. Keep the scraper blade flat and gently remove buildup without gouging the surface.
3. Use small amounts of water, scrape again, and wipe with a clean cotton towel.
4. Repeat rather than forcing the scraper into a stubborn patch.
5. Dry thoroughly and restore a thin coat of grapeseed oil.

For residue that will not lift, consult the manufacturer's guidance before using abrasive pads or deep-cleaning products.

## Re-Seasoning with Grapeseed Oil

1. Begin with a clean, completely dry cooking surface.
2. Spread a **very thin, even layer** of grapeseed oil across the griddle.
3. Heat according to Traeger's seasoning instructions so the oil bonds to the surface.
4. Allow to cool safely. Repeat thin coats as needed to rebuild seasoning.

**Several thin coats are preferable to one thick, sticky coat.**

## Before Cooking

1. Inspect the surface for debris, stickiness, or residue.
2. Preheat to the temperature appropriate for the food.
3. Wipe the warm surface with a clean cotton towel if needed.
4. Add only the oil needed for cooking.

## Products and Practices to Avoid

- **Microfiber cloths on a hot surface:** synthetic fibers may melt.
- Thick oil applications that leave sticky buildup.
- Damaged scraper blades or aggressive scraping that gouges the surface.
- Large amounts of cold water on an extremely hot griddle.
- Household cleaners or abrasive products not approved for the cooking surface.

## Preventing Residue from Transferring to Food

- Scrape between batches, especially after cheese, sauces, or sugary marinades.
- Wipe away loosened residue before adding the next food.
- Use fresh cotton towels rather than spreading old grease around.
- Maintain a thin, even seasoning layer.
- If dark flakes transfer to food, stop and clean the surface before continuing.

## Notes

- **Grapeseed oil** is the preferred seasoning oil in Marco's Kitchen.
- This house guide supplements, rather than replaces, the Traeger owner's manual.
