# Hawaiian Pineapple Grilled Chicken

**Status:** ⭐ Gold Star  
**Yield:** 4-6 servings  
**Category:** Mains

## Ingredients

### Chicken

- 2 lb (about 900 g) boneless, skinless chicken thighs

### Pineapple Marinade

- 180 g pineapple juice
- 60 g soy sauce
- 30 g brown sugar
- 30 g neutral oil
- 15 g rice vinegar
- 3 garlic cloves, finely grated or minced
- 10 g fresh ginger, finely grated
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

### Optional Finishing

- Sliced scallions
- Grilled pineapple
- Lime wedges

## Method

1. Combine the pineapple juice, soy sauce, brown sugar, oil, rice vinegar, garlic, ginger, and black pepper.
2. Reserve a small clean portion for brushing or serving if desired.
3. Add the chicken thighs to the remaining marinade and coat thoroughly.
4. Refrigerate and marinate for **2-4 hours**. Do not leave the chicken in fresh pineapple marinade overnight.
5. Remove the chicken from the marinade and let the excess drip off.
6. Discard the used marinade.
7. Grill over medium-high to high heat, turning as needed, until well browned with lightly caramelized edges.
8. Cook until the thighs are safely cooked through and tender.
9. If using reserved marinade as a glaze, brush it on toward the end of cooking so the sugars do not burn.
10. Rest briefly, then serve with optional scallions, grilled pineapple, and lime.

## Cooking

- **Method:** Grill
- **Heat:** Medium-high to high
- **Target:** Caramelized exterior with juicy chicken thighs
- **Minimum Internal Temperature:** 165°F

## Notes

- Pineapple contains bromelain, an enzyme that tenderizes meat. Long exposure to fresh pineapple juice can make the exterior of the chicken mushy, so keep the marinade to roughly 2-4 hours.
- Canned or heat-treated pineapple juice has much less active bromelain and is more forgiving for longer marination.
- Chicken thighs work particularly well because they remain juicy over high grill heat.
- Because the marinade contains sugar, watch the chicken carefully once it begins browning.
- Never use marinade that has contacted raw chicken as a finishing sauce unless it has been thoroughly boiled first.
