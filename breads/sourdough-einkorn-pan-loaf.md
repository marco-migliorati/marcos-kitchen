# Sourdough Einkorn Pan Loaf

**Status:** ⭐ Gold Star  
**Last Tested:** October 9, 2026  
**Yield:** 1 loaf  
**Category:** Breads  
**Version:** 2 - Approved loaf; revised fermentation schedule pending validation

Adapted from the Gold Star Sourdough Pan Loaf to incorporate Jovial all-purpose and whole wheat einkorn flour. The first bake delivered excellent flavor and a softer, preferred crust. The fermentation schedule below is the plan for the next bake and has not yet been validated.

## Levain

- 18 g active sourdough starter
- 18 g bread flour
- 18 g Jovial whole wheat einkorn flour
- 36 g water

**Total levain:** 90 g

Feed the starter. Approximately 4 hours later, begin the autolyse, provided the levain is active and approaching readiness.

## Dough

- 225 g bread flour
- 180 g Jovial all-purpose einkorn flour
- 45 g Jovial whole wheat einkorn flour
- 320 g water, approximately 95°F
- 9 g fine sea salt
- All 90 g ripe levain

**Approximate total dough weight:** 869 g  
**Einkorn proportion:** 50% of main-dough flour (225 g of 450 g); approximately 49% of total flour when the levain is included, assuming the starter is made with conventional wheat flour at 100% hydration.

## Method

### Levain and Autolyse

1. Feed the starter to build the levain.
2. Approximately 4 hours later, combine the bread flour, all-purpose einkorn flour, and whole wheat einkorn flour with **all 320 g water** at approximately 95°F.
3. Mix until no dry flour remains.
4. Cover and rest for **30 minutes**.

### Mix

5. Add all the ripe levain and mix gently until incorporated.
6. Cover and rest for 15 minutes.
7. Add the salt and work it gently into the dough.
8. Return the dough to its container. Do not perform the original 20 aggressive slap-and-fold movements.

### Folds and Bulk Fermentation

9. Perform **four gentle folds**, resting 30 minutes between folds 1-2, 2-3, and 3-4. Avoid tearing the dough.
10. After the fourth fold, cover and leave undisturbed for **approximately 2 hours as a starting estimate**, checking the dough's development rather than relying solely on the clock.
11. For the next test, aim for approximately **50-75% increase in volume**, with a puffy, aerated appearance. Do not insist on doubling if the dough already looks ready. Record the actual rise and timing.

### Shape and Pan

12. Grease one loaf pan thoroughly.
13. Gently fold and shape the dough into a bun or loaf without tearing it.
14. Place the dough seam-side down in the pan.
15. If the dough appears tight and underproofed, allow a short covered room-temperature rest for visible expansion; otherwise refrigerate promptly. Record any rest time.

### Cold Proof

16. Cover securely and refrigerate **overnight, approximately 12-16 hours**. This is the planned next test, not the schedule used for the first successful bake.
17. Bake directly from the refrigerator if the dough appears adequately proofed. If it still looks dense and tight, assess whether additional proofing is needed before baking.

## Baking - Florida Convection Oven

1. Preheat the oven to **450°F convection**.
2. Place the covered loaf pan in the oven and bake for **30 minutes**.
3. Remove the cover and reduce the temperature to **425°F convection**.
4. Bake uncovered for **12 minutes**.
5. Rotate the pan and bake for up to **another 12 minutes**, checking color and doneness.
6. Aim for an internal temperature of approximately **205-210°F**, while also checking that the loaf is fully set. Adjust time if needed.
7. Remove from the pan and cool completely on a rack before slicing.

## Equipment

- Large mixing bowl or dough tub
- Digital scale
- Dough scraper
- One loaf pan and oven-safe cover
- Cooling rack
- Instant-read thermometer

## Notes

- Add all water during the autolyse; none is reserved.
- Einkorn has a more delicate gluten structure than modern bread wheat. Handle gently and use the pan to support the loaf.
- The permanent formula uses bread flour and Jovial einkorn flours. The first bake used a small temporary substitution of regular all-purpose flour for bread flour and conventional whole wheat flour where available; this does not change the canonical formula.
- The Gold Star rating reflects the **flavor and eating quality of the first bake**, not validation of the new overnight-proof schedule.
- Keep the original Gold Star Sourdough Pan Loaf as a separate recipe.

## Results

### Test 1 - October 9, 2026

- **Fermentation deviation:** Dough was accidentally left on the counter approximately **8 hours at 73°F** overnight after folding.
- **Next morning:** Dough appeared roughly the same size and felt normal when shaped into a bun and placed in the loaf pan.
- **Cold proof:** Refrigerated briefly before baking, rather than overnight.
- **Baking:** Covered at 450°F convection, then uncovered at 425°F convection.
- **Oven spring:** Less than the original sourdough pan loaf, but the finished loaf retained a rounded top.
- **Crust:** Even golden color, less aggressive browning, and a softer texture that was preferred over the original loaf.
- **Shape:** Some horizontal splitting along the lower side.
- **Crumb:** Less bubbly / tighter than the original.
- **Flavor:** Amazing.
- **Verdict:** ⭐ Gold Star for flavor and softer crust.

### Next Bake - Validation Plan

- Follow the intended shorter bulk fermentation: begin checking after approximately 2 hours following the final fold and aim for 50-75% rise, adjusting by dough appearance.
- Refrigerate overnight for approximately 12-16 hours.
- Compare loaf height, side splitting, crumb openness, crust softness, and flavor with Test 1.
- Record actual dough temperature, bulk duration, rise, and proof duration before further recipe changes.
