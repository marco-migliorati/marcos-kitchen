# Negroni

**Status:** ⭐ Gold Star  
**Yield:** 1 cocktail  
**Category:** Cocktails

## Ingredients

- 1 oz gin
- 1 oz Campari
- 1 oz quality sweet red vermouth
- Orange peel, for garnish

## Method

1. Add the gin, Campari, and sweet vermouth to a mixing glass filled with ice.
2. Stir until thoroughly chilled and properly diluted, about 20-30 seconds.
3. Strain over one large cube in a rocks glass.
4. Express an orange peel over the surface of the drink.
5. Rub the peel around the rim of the glass and use it as the garnish.

## Ingredients Matter

A Negroni is equal parts, so there is nowhere for a weak ingredient to hide.

### Gin

Use a classic, juniper-forward London Dry style. The gin needs enough structure to stand up to Campari and vermouth.

### Vermouth

Use **sweet red vermouth**, not dry vermouth.

The vermouth is especially important. Once opened, refrigerate it and treat it like wine rather than shelf-stable liquor. Fresh vermouth makes a noticeable difference in a Negroni.

### Campari

Campari supplies the characteristic bitter orange and herbal backbone.

## Technique

- Stir rather than shake.
- Use plenty of ice while stirring.
- Proper dilution is part of the recipe; an under-diluted Negroni can taste harsh and disjointed.
- A large cube in the serving glass slows further dilution.
- Expressing the orange peel over the finished drink adds aromatic orange oils before the first sip.

## Ratio

The canonical ratio is easy to remember:

**1 gin : 1 Campari : 1 sweet vermouth**

## Notes

- The original Marco's Kitchen Negroni was judged only average; the vermouth was identified as the weakest ingredient.
- Upgrading the vermouth and keeping it refrigerated after opening are the primary improvements.
- Fresh, high-quality ingredients matter disproportionately in such a simple cocktail.
