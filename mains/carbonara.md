# Carbonara

**Status:** ⭐ Gold Star  
**Yield:** 4 servings  
**Category:** Mains

## Ingredients

- 400 g spaghetti or rigatoni
- 150 g guanciale, cut into small strips or cubes
- 4 large egg yolks
- 2 large whole eggs
- 100 g Pecorino Romano, finely grated, plus more for serving
- Freshly ground black pepper, generously
- Kosher salt, for the pasta water

## Method

1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Salt it more lightly than usual because the guanciale and Pecorino are both salty.
2. Add the guanciale to a cold skillet, then place over medium-low to medium heat.
3. Cook slowly until the fat has rendered and the guanciale is browned and crisp around the edges.
4. Remove the skillet from the heat, leaving the rendered fat in the pan.
5. In a bowl, whisk together the egg yolks, whole eggs, Pecorino Romano, and a generous amount of freshly ground black pepper. The mixture will be thick.
6. Cook the pasta until just shy of al dente.
7. Reserve at least 1 cup of pasta water before draining.
8. Add the hot drained pasta to the skillet with the guanciale and rendered fat. Toss well.
9. Allow the pan to cool briefly so it is hot but no longer over direct heat.
10. Add the egg and cheese mixture and toss vigorously.
11. Add reserved pasta water a little at a time while tossing until the sauce becomes glossy, creamy, and coats the pasta.
12. Return the guanciale fully through the pasta if any was held aside.
13. Serve immediately with additional Pecorino Romano and black pepper.

## Technique

- The sauce is an emulsion of egg, cheese, rendered guanciale fat, and starchy pasta water.
- Do not add the egg mixture over direct heat. Excess heat will scramble the eggs.
- Add pasta water gradually. It is easier to loosen the sauce than to recover from adding too much water.
- Work quickly once the pasta is drained.
- The finished sauce should be glossy and creamy without cream.

## Notes

- Guanciale is preferred. Pancetta can substitute when necessary, but bacon changes the flavor considerably.
- Pecorino Romano is the traditional cheese and provides both salt and sharpness.
- There is no cream in the canonical version.
- Salt the pasta water conservatively.
- Inspired by the Basics with Babish carbonara that originally led to this Marco's Kitchen version.
