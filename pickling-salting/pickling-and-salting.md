# Pickling & Salting Quick Reference

Useful prep techniques for Marco's Kitchen.

## Quick-Pickled Shallots

A softer, more subtle alternative to raw onion in salads, sandwiches, bowls, and grilled dishes.

### Ingredients

- 1 shallot, thinly sliced
- 60 g (1/4 cup) red wine vinegar
- 1/4 tsp kosher salt
- Small pinch sugar, optional

### Method

1. Thinly slice the shallot.
2. Combine with the red wine vinegar, salt, and optional sugar.
3. Refrigerate for at least 15-30 minutes. For a softer, more thoroughly pickled shallot, leave refrigerated for several hours or throughout the day.
4. Drain before using.
5. Reserve the pickling liquid when useful as an acidic component in a dressing.

### Notes

- Longer pickling softens both the texture and pungency.
- Keep refrigerated while pickling.
- For Horiatiki Salad, pickle the shallot throughout the day and drain before assembly.
- Do not add the cucumber to this pickle when making Horiatiki. Keeping the cucumber out of the acid preserves its fresh crunch.

## Salted Cucumbers

Salting seasons cucumber while drawing off excess water, helping salads stay flavorful rather than becoming diluted.

### Method

1. Cut the cucumber as required by the recipe.
2. Season lightly and evenly with kosher salt.
3. Let stand for 10-15 minutes.
4. Drain off the released liquid.
5. Add the cucumber to the finished dish.

### Notes

- The goal is to remove some excess water, not cure or pickle the cucumber.
- Keep the salting time short enough to preserve a fresh, crisp texture.
- For Horiatiki Salad, salt the cucumber separately while the other ingredients are being prepared.
- Taste before adding additional salt to the finished dish.

## When to Use Which Technique

**Quick-pickle:** Use when an ingredient benefits from softened pungency, added acidity, and a lightly pickled character.

**Salt and drain:** Use when an ingredient contains enough water to dilute the finished dish but you still want its fresh flavor and texture.
