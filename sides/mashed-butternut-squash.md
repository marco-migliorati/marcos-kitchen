# Mashed Butternut Squash

A rich, savory mashed butternut squash with plenty of butter, milk, and a touch of garlic. Brown butter is an optional upgrade for an even deeper, nuttier flavor.

## Ingredients

- 1 medium butternut squash (about 2 1/2–3 lb)
- 4–6 tbsp butter
- 4–6 tbsp whole milk
- 1 small clove garlic, finely grated or minced
- 1/2–3/4 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- Small pinch of nutmeg (optional)

### Brown Butter Option

For extra flavor, brown the butter before adding it to the squash:

1. Melt the butter in a small saucepan over medium heat.
2. Continue cooking, swirling occasionally, until the milk solids turn golden brown and the butter smells nutty.
3. Remove from the heat immediately and use in place of the regular melted butter.

Optional: Add a few fresh sage leaves while the butter browns. Let them crisp, then crumble them over the finished squash.

## Instructions

1. **Heat the oven to 425°F.**

2. **Prepare the squash.**  
   Cut the butternut squash in half lengthwise and scoop out the seeds. Lightly oil the cut surfaces.

3. **Roast.**  
   Place the squash cut-side down on a parchment-lined sheet pan. Roast for 40–50 minutes, or until completely tender when pierced with a knife.

4. **Scoop.**  
   Let the squash cool just enough to handle, then scoop the flesh into a large bowl.

5. **Mash.**  
   Add the butter, milk, garlic, salt, and black pepper. Mash with a potato masher or fork until smooth. Start with 4 tablespoons each of butter and milk and add more as needed. For a richer version, use the full 6 tablespoons of each.

6. **Finish.**  
   Taste and adjust the salt and pepper. Add a small pinch of nutmeg if desired.

## Notes

- Roasting instead of boiling keeps the squash from becoming watery and concentrates its natural sweetness.
- The amount of milk needed will vary depending on the moisture in the squash.
- For the brown butter version, start with less milk and adjust after mashing so the squash stays rich rather than loose.
- Brown butter and fresh sage make an especially good fall variation.
