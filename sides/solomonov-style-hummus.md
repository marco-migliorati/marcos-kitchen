# Solomonov-Style Hummus

**Status:** ⭐ Gold Star  
**Yield:** About 3 cups  
**Category:** Sauces & Condiments

## Ingredients

### Chickpeas

- 250 g dried chickpeas
- 1 tsp baking soda
- Water, for soaking and cooking

### Hummus

- 300 g tahini
- 120 g fresh lemon juice
- 4 garlic cloves
- 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
- About 120-180 g ice-cold water, as needed
- Cooked chickpeas from above

### For Serving

- Extra-virgin olive oil
- Paprika, cumin, or za'atar, optional
- Chopped parsley, optional

## Method

### Prepare the Chickpeas

1. Cover the dried chickpeas generously with water and soak overnight.
2. Drain the soaked chickpeas.
3. Add the chickpeas and baking soda to a pot and briefly heat while stirring.
4. Cover generously with fresh water.
5. Simmer until the chickpeas are extremely tender and beginning to fall apart. They should be softer than chickpeas intended for salads.
6. Drain.

### Make the Tahini Sauce

7. Combine the tahini, lemon juice, garlic, and salt.
8. Blend or whisk while gradually adding ice-cold water.
9. Continue adding water until the tahini becomes pale, smooth, creamy, and loose enough to blend easily with the chickpeas.

### Make the Hummus

10. Add the very soft, warm chickpeas to a food processor.
11. Process until smooth.
12. Add the prepared tahini sauce and continue processing until the hummus is exceptionally smooth, light, and creamy.
13. If needed, add additional ice-cold water a little at a time until the desired texture is reached.
14. Taste and adjust the lemon and salt.
15. Spoon into a shallow bowl and finish with extra-virgin olive oil and any optional garnishes.

## Notes

- The defining characteristic is an unusually high proportion of tahini and an exceptionally smooth texture.
- Cook the chickpeas much softer than you think necessary. They should be nearly falling apart.
- Baking soda helps soften the chickpeas and their skins.
- The tahini may initially tighten or seize when lemon juice is added. Cold water loosens it into a pale, creamy sauce.
- Add water based on texture rather than treating the quantity as absolute.
- Serve at room temperature rather than refrigerator-cold for the best texture and flavor.
- This is the Marco's Kitchen working version inspired by Michael Solomonov's hummus technique, not a transcription of a published recipe.
