# Spaghetti Aglio, Olio e Peperoncino

**Status:** 🧪 Testing  
**Last Tested:** Not yet tested  
**Yield:** 2 servings  
**Category:** Mains

## Ingredients

- 200 g spaghetti
- 45–55 g extra virgin olive oil (3–4 tbsp)
- 3–4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- ¼–½ tsp dried Italian peperoncino or crushed red pepper flakes
- Salt, for the pasta water
- Approximately 250 ml reserved starchy pasta water, as needed
- Fresh Italian parsley, finely chopped (optional)

## Method

### 1. Cook the Pasta

1. Bring a pot of water to a boil and salt moderately.
2. Use only enough water to comfortably cook the spaghetti. Less water produces a higher concentration of starch.
3. Cook the spaghetti approximately 2 minutes short of al dente.
4. Reserve at least 250 ml of pasta water before draining, or transfer the spaghetti directly to the skillet.

### 2. Infuse the Olive Oil

1. Add the olive oil, sliced garlic, and peperoncino to a **cold, wide skillet**.
2. Turn the heat to low.
3. Slowly warm the oil, allowing the garlic to release its flavor.
4. Look for small, gentle bubbles around the garlic, not vigorous sizzling.
5. Continue for approximately 4–6 minutes, adjusting the heat as needed.
6. When the garlic becomes fragrant and just begins to turn pale gold, add approximately 60 ml (¼ cup) of pasta water to stop the frying.

**Important:** The goal is garlic-infused oil, not browned or crispy garlic. Gentle cooking prevents bitterness.

**Safety:** Add the pasta water carefully to avoid splattering hot oil.

### 3. Create the Emulsion

1. Transfer the slightly undercooked spaghetti into the skillet.
2. Add another 60 ml (¼ cup) of reserved pasta water.
3. Increase the heat to medium or medium-low, maintaining a gentle simmer.
4. Toss or stir the spaghetti continuously for approximately 1–2 minutes.
5. Add more pasta water in small increments whenever the sauce becomes too thick or dry.
6. Continue tossing until the spaghetti is al dente and the sauce becomes glossy and lightly creamy.
7. Remove from the heat when the sauce coats the spaghetti without leaving a puddle of oil or water.

### 4. Finish and Serve

1. Taste and adjust the salt if necessary.
2. Add finely chopped parsley, if using.
3. Optionally finish with a small drizzle of fresh extra virgin olive oil.
4. Serve immediately.

## Baking / Cooking

- **Garlic temperature:** Low heat, gentle bubbling without aggressive frying.
- **Garlic cooking time:** Approximately 4–6 minutes, depending on the pan and heat.
- **Pasta cooking time:** Approximately 2 minutes less than package directions before transferring to the skillet.
- **Finishing time:** Approximately 1–2 minutes in the skillet, or until al dente.
- **Target temperature:** Not applicable. Judge by garlic color, pasta texture, and sauce consistency.

## Equipment

- Large pot for cooking spaghetti
- Wide skillet or sauté pan
- Tongs
- Ladle or heatproof measuring cup
- Sharp knife for thinly slicing garlic

## Notes

### Garlic Temperature Control

Starting with cold olive oil and garlic allows the garlic to infuse slowly without immediately frying.

The addition of pasta water lowers the oil temperature and helps prevent further browning.

Garlic that is lightly golden is acceptable, but dark brown garlic can introduce bitterness.

### Understanding the Emulsion

The creaminess comes from combining three elements:

1. **Olive oil:** Provides richness and carries the garlic and chili flavors.
2. **Starchy pasta water:** Supplies water and starch, which helps stabilize the sauce.
3. **Mechanical movement:** Tossing breaks the oil into tiny droplets that disperse throughout the water.

Gentle heat evaporates excess water, concentrating the sauce until it coats the spaghetti.

No butter, cream, or cheese is required.

### Troubleshooting

**Sauce is too oily:**
- Add a small splash of starchy pasta water.
- Toss vigorously to bring the emulsion together.

**Sauce is too watery:**
- Continue tossing over gentle heat to evaporate excess moisture.
- Avoid adding more oil.

**Sauce is too thick or sticky:**
- Add a small amount of pasta water and toss.

**Garlic is browning too quickly:**
- Reduce the heat immediately.
- Add a small amount of pasta water to stop the frying.

**Sauce separates:**
- Add a splash of pasta water and toss vigorously.
- Avoid allowing the pan to become completely dry.

### Peperoncino

Dried Italian peperoncino provides traditional heat.

Crushed red pepper flakes are a suitable substitute.

Add the chili to the olive oil during the garlic infusion so the capsaicin distributes throughout the oil.

Start with ¼ teaspoon and increase according to preference.

### Key Technique

**Infuse → Cool → Emulsify → Reduce → Serve**

1. Infuse the oil gently with garlic and chili.
2. Cool the oil with starchy pasta water before the garlic browns.
3. Emulsify through continuous tossing.
4. Reduce excess water with gentle heat.
5. Serve when the sauce is glossy and coats the pasta.
