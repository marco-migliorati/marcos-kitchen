# Sourdough Schiacciata

**Status:** 🧪 Testing  
**Yield:** 1 sheet-pan schiacciata  
**Category:** Breads

## Starter Build

- 25 g mature 100%-hydration starter
- 50 g flour
- 50 g water

Mix and let rise until active and near peak. This produces 125 g starter. Use 100 g in the dough and retain 25 g.

## Ingredients

- 500 g bread flour
- 325 g water
- 100 g active 100%-hydration sourdough starter
- 12 g fine sea salt
- Extra-virgin olive oil, generously, for the pan and top
- Coarse or flaky sea salt, for finishing

## Method

1. Combine the water and active starter.
2. Add the bread flour and mix until no dry flour remains.
3. Rest for about 30 minutes.
4. Add the salt and mix until fully incorporated.
5. Perform 3 folds at 30-minute intervals.
6. Continue bulk fermentation until the dough is clearly risen, aerated, and active.
7. Generously oil a sheet pan with extra-virgin olive oil.
8. Transfer the dough to the pan and gently stretch it toward the edges without forcing it.
9. Let the dough relax, then stretch again as needed to fill the pan.
10. Proof in the pan for about 2 hours, until visibly puffy and relaxed.
11. Heat the oven to 425-450°F.
12. Dimple the dough thoroughly with your fingertips.
13. Drizzle generously with extra-virgin olive oil and finish with coarse or flaky sea salt.
14. Bake until deeply golden with a crisp, well-browned bottom and top.
15. Finish with additional olive oil after baking.
16. Cool enough to set the crumb before splitting horizontally for sandwiches.

## Target

The benchmark is the sandwich schiacciata from La Schiaccia dell'Opio in Montepulciano:

- Crisp, olive-oil-rich exterior
- Soft, moderately open interior
- Thin enough for sandwiches rather than thick focaccia
- Strong enough to split horizontally and hold fillings

## Notes

- Total dough hydration is about 68.2% after accounting for the flour and water in the starter.
- The recipe deliberately targets sandwich-style Tuscan schiacciata rather than thick focaccia.
- Be generous with olive oil in the pan and on top.
- This is a sourdough conversion and remains 🧪 Testing until baked and compared with the Montepulciano benchmark.
